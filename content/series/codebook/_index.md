Three posts about one thing: the context I share with an agent.

They belong together because each one makes the next possible:
the first measures what shared context costs,
the second builds a codebook out of my own transcripts,
and the third finds that this codebook is not a convenience for someone else but access.

{{< codebuchschema >}}

## Whether this is for you

The chain from left to right is the story: measure, abbreviate, hand on.

**The two arrows to the upper and lower right** are the reason to read.
The same shortcut saves two entirely different things — keystrokes on a keyboard and round-trips in a conversation — and the two savings have nothing to do with each other.
Two completely different arguments lead to the same optimisation.
That is the claim of the series.

> If you enjoy watching a plausible explanation break against a measurement, you are in the right place.
> The first part opens with three dead agents and a cause that turned out not to be one.

**The arrow going back** is the uncomfortable half.
A codebook you write down once goes stale: the corpus keeps growing, and yesterday's good abbreviations are not tomorrow's.

Nothing is assumed but curiosity about how you work.
The first part deals in token counts and timestamps, the second in a frequency list, the third in no numbers at all.
If you only read one, read the third — it stands on its own.

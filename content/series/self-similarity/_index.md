Four posts about a single question: who is actually writing here?

They hang together the way a claim hangs together with its evidence.
The first explains why this blog's cadence says nothing about its speed,
the second where the material comes from (mechanical calculators, cellular automata, two books),
the third carries the actual claim: what holds for chaotic systems holds between agents too,
and the fourth sets out what share of these texts belongs to Claude.

The title is meant literally: the same pattern turns up again one level out.

{{< autorschema >}}

## Whether this is for you

The two boxes at the top are the ordinary part: where the material comes from and how fast it drains away are questions you can ask of any blog.

**The way down** is the reason to read.
It carries the claim the series is named after: sufficiently complex agents are a complex system, and there the regularities described elsewhere long ago apply.
Four of them can be measured on your own machine, and part 3 shows how.

> If you can take predator-prey models and agents in the same sentence, you are in the right place.
> If an analogy between ecology and software goes too far for you, less so.

**The box at the bottom** is the question everything runs towards.
The answer is "partly", and what makes it interesting is that the parts come apart cleanly: the material is mine, the form is mostly Claude's.
The seam shows most clearly in a sentence that does not survive translation.

No mathematics is assumed.
Cellular automata, a Rubik's cube and a calculating machine all appear, and all three are explained in passing.
The fourth part stands on its own, if the only question you care about is how much of this is mine.

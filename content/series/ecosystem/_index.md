Four posts about a claim I made in September, and about what others have measured since.

The claim was in [Predator, prey, deception](/posts/predator-prey-and-deception/): several agents form a complex system, and familiar regularities hold there without anyone wanting them to.
The post ended with the advice to treat a setup of several agents like a small ecosystem.
Hence the name of this series.

The first part is the cross-check: three texts, four patterns, and which of them hold.
The next three each take one text in detail:
the second a study in which the same model never intervenes alone and almost always does in threes,
the third a simulation in which populations made of a single model unlearn disagreement,
the fourth an incident report in which the check was missing because something was meant to be measured.

## Is this for you?

**If you read the post on predator and prey**, this is the reckoning.
Not everything held.
None of the three texts shows an oscillation, and one sentence about intent was too strong.

> If you like watching a thesis measured against someone else's numbers, you are in the right place.
> If you are after a warning about the machine, probably not: this is about mechanisms, not scenarios.

Nothing is assumed.
Every number comes from a linked source, and where a source reports on itself, that is said too.
